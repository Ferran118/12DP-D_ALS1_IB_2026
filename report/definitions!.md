### DEFINITIONS (Project Disease) ###

**SOD1 (Superoxide Dismutase 1)** is a crucial antioxidant enzyme responsible for protecting cells from oxidative stress. When mutations occur in its gene, the proteins misfold and form toxic aggregates, which is a core pathogenic mechanism in certain types of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).
  
**Anterograde and retrograde transport** are the two directions in which materials are moved back and forth along an axon inside a neuron (nerve cell). Because neurons can be extremely long, they rely on these active transport systems—powered by motor proteins moving along microscopic tracks called microtubules—to distribute essential supplies.

A **motor unit** is a functional team made up of a single motor neuron (a nerve cell located in the spinal cord or brainstem) and all of the individual skeletal muscle fibers that it controls and signals to contract.
When a motor neuron fires an electrical signal, every single muscle fiber in that specific motor unit contracts together.

**Age of onset**: patient’s age when those first symptoms become noticeable.
**Onset**: start of the symptoms.

**Quadriparesis** is a medical term that refers to weakness in all four limbs (both arms and both legs).
"Quadri-" means four.
"-paresis" means weakness or incomplete paralysis (as opposed to -plegia, which means complete paralysis or the total inability to move).
While a person with quadriparesis experiences significant muscle weakness in all four limbs, they still retain some degree of movement or control over them.
