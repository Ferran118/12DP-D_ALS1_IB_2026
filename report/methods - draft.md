# METHODS
## Data Sources
To investigate the molecular basis of the disorder, we retrieved the complete wild-type coding sequence (CDS) and amino acid sequence of human superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1) from the NCBI Nucleotide and Gene databases (RefSeq NM_000454.5, Gene ID 6647). We gathered genetic, locus, and phenotypic annotations linked to Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis 1 (ALS1) through OMIM (#105400) and UniProtKB (UniProt ID: P00441, Disease ID: DI-00108). Functional details regarding human Cu/Zn-SOD enzymatic activity were documented using key literature from PubMed (PMID: 18948262, PMID: 24140062, PMID: 36630448). To compare evolutionary conservation across mammals, we collected orthologous CDS and protein sequences for Pongo abelii (Gene ID: 100170281, RefSeq NM_001131969.1) and Bos taurus (Gene ID: 281495) from NCBI Gene. Finally, 3D atomic coordinates for the wild-type human SOD1 homodimer were downloaded from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 2C9V).   

## Implementation
We performed a pairwise sequence alignment between the wild-type SOD1 CDS and the query mutant sequence using BLASTn under default settings. The identified c.14C>T nucleotide substitution was translated in silico using the standard genetic code to determine its effect on the reading frame. For comparative evolutionary analysis, we aligned the CDS and amino acid sequences of Homo sapiens, Pongo abelii, and Bos taurus using Clustal Omega. We characterized the functional domains of the mature 154-amino-acid polypeptide, mapping the catalytic copper (Cu²⁺) site, the structural zinc (Zn²⁺) site, the intramolecular disulfide bridge, and the anti-parallel β-barrel motif. Lastly, we modeled the target p.Ala5Val substitution on the PDB 2C9V structure to evaluate changes in side-chain volume, spatial packing, and structural strain at the homodimer interface.   

## Statistical Analysis
Sequence alignments and similarity metrics obtained via BLASTn were assessed using Query Cover percentage, Percent Identity, and Expectation values (E-value), establishing statistical significance at an E-value threshold of 0.0. We evaluated amino acid conservation and substitution plausibility across mammalian orthologs using the BLOSUM62 matrix, scoring the log-odds ratios to distinguish conservative from non-conservative replacements.
